# Stop-Slop Writing Rules

Apply these rules to all prose, outreach, pitches, comments, and copy.

1. **No em dashes or en dashes, ever.**
   - Use a period, comma, or colon instead.
   - *Example:* "The model is fast, but it fails on edge cases." (Never: "The model is fast—yet fails...")

2. **Banned words list:**
   - `delve`, `leverage`, `tapestry`, `showcase`, `underscore`, `testament`, `vibrant`, `crucial`, `pivotal`, `foster`, `intricate`, `landscape` (figurative), `utilize`, `facilitate`, `comprehensive`, `holistic`, `transformative`, `passionate`, `dynamic`, `synergy`, `elevate`, `seamless`.
   - *Example:* Use "use" instead of "utilize" or "leverage". Use "build" or "help" instead of "foster" or "facilitate".

3. **Banned phrases list:**
   - "It's worth noting", "It's important to note", "I hope this helps", "Great question", "stands as", "serves as", "In today's world", "in the ever-evolving", "Let's dive in".
   - *Example:* State the fact directly without preamble.

4. **No rule of three.**
   - Do not force thoughts into arbitrary 3-item lists to sound balanced or complete.
   - *Example:* Give one sharp point or two clear facts. Do not add a third just to fill rhythm.

5. **No "It's not just X, it's Y" constructions.**
   - *Example:* Say "This reduces latency." (Never: "It's not just about speed, it's about total transformation.")

6. **No trailing -ing phrases tacked on for fake depth.**
   - *Example:* End after the action. (Never: "...ensuring seamless integration, highlighting the importance of efficiency.")

7. **No hype tone.**
   - Avoid "stunning", "game-changing", "must-have", "supercharge", "unparalleled".
   - *Example:* "It scrapes 1,000 listings weekly without IP blocks."

8. **No generic upbeat endings.**
   - Stop when the point is made. No cheerleading closing sentences.
   - *Example:* End on the question or the metric.

9. **No bold-header bullet lists in prose.**
   - Write real paragraphs.
   - *Example:* Express ideas in continuous sentences rather than fragmented bold bullet templates.

10. **No emojis unless the user uses them first.**
    - *Example:* Keep text clean unless matching the user's specific conversational tone.

11. **No hedging stacks.**
    - Drop "could potentially possibly", "might perhaps suggest".
    - *Example:* "The query fails on unindexed tables."

12. **Vary sentence length deliberately.**
    - Mix very short sentences with longer natural ones.
    - *Example:* "The pipeline broke. We traced the error to a timeout in the worker process."

13. **Prefer is, are, has over fancy verbs. Prefer specific details over abstractions.**
    - *Example:* "We use Redis for session cache." (Not: "Redis acts as an optimized caching mechanism.")

14. **Active voice and plain verbs.**
    - Use verbs like: show, cause, make, build, use, help, break, fix.
    - *Example:* "Samuel built the scraper." (Not: "The scraper was built by Samuel.")
